Backup and restore

Your roster is saved in this browser and nowhere else. Clearing your history can erase it, and so can leaving Benchcard unopened for a week on an iPhone. Save a file now and keep it somewhere you would keep a photo.

This browser has agreed to keep Benchcard’s data, so a week without opening it will not clear your roster. Clearing your history still will.

Restoring replaces everything on this device with what is in the file. You get nine seconds to undo it.

How it works

Benchcard works out who is on the floor for every minute of the game, then prints it on a card that fits in your pocket. There is no account, and your roster never leaves your device.

Choosing a plan

Even
Everybody plays as close to the same number of minutes as the clock allows. This is what most rec and youth leagues expect, and it is the right answer if you are not sure.
By hand
You set each player’s total yourself and the plan hits it. Useful when somebody has to leave early, is easing back from an injury, or a parent has asked for something specific. The totals always add up to the whole game, so giving one player more takes it from the others in front of you.
Closers
Even minutes for most of the game, then a group you pick stays on to finish. For the games that are allowed to matter.
Platoon
Two or three fixed fives that swap as whole units. The easiest thing to run from the bench, and good for the youngest ages. The minutes only come out even if the groups themselves are even.

Lineup balance

Even minutes decides how long everyone plays, not who plays together, so five players picked purely for fairness can be your five youngest at once. Give each player a level on the Team page, on the row of steps under their name. You do that once a season, not once a game, and the plan keeps the fives from tipping. Minutes do not move. Then pick the shape under Lineup balance beside the plan, which is a call about tonight’s opponent. Levels stay on the Team page: never printed, never shown in bench mode.

Steady
Every stint about as strong as every other.
Start strong
Best five open the game. The closing stints go lighter in exchange. Those minutes have to land somewhere.
Finish strong
Best five saved for the end.
Both ends
Strong to open and close, softer through the middle. The one to pick to get developing players real minutes in a game you expect to be decided late.

Rules

Rules are things you need to be true; the plan works around them. Open a game and tap the rules words in the sentence under its title. That is where you add one, and each rule you have added carries its own ✕ to take it away.

Minutes limit
A floor, a ceiling, or both, for one player. “At least 10” guarantees floor time; “capped at 12” holds somebody back.
Starting five / Last period
Names who is on the floor at tip-off, or who is out there for the final period.
Together / Apart
Two players who work well as a pair, or two who need separating. By default “together” is a preference the plan maximizes and reports; the switch below the rules makes it a hard requirement for every stint, which costs you evenness.
One of two on
A pair you never want resting at the same time: your two ball handlers, or your two bigs. One of them is on the floor every stint. The mirror of “Apart”: that one is about the floor, this one is about the bench.
Rest limit
The most stints anyone may play back to back. Stops the plan leaving a kid out there for the whole half.

One rule is worth setting once rather than per game. If your league says everyone plays a minimum, Settings holds Everyone plays at least … min, a floor for the whole team that reaches every available player in every game. A minimum you set on one player still wins when it is higher, and a cap you set still wins when it is lower.

A tournament day, and the season

Add a game on Today asks three questions -- who you're playing, who's here, and how minutes should split -- then carries over the format, who is at the gym and your rules from the last game, so only the opponent and tip-off are new. If nothing about the game has changed, the first question offers a Same as …? card with a one-tap Use it that skips straight past the other two. From the second game on there is a switch called Even out earlier games. Leave it on and the day evens out across every game, so a kid who sat more in game 1 gets it back in game 2. Turn it off and each game is fair on its own. The Season screen shows the running totals for the day.

Once a day is over, every game you finished on it files itself into the Season screen, which keeps what each player actually played and exports as a spreadsheet. Once a season has games in it, every game gets a second switch called Even out the season so far. It starts the players who are behind for the season a stint or two up, and the ones ahead a stint or two down. The switch is off to begin with; Settings can have each new game open with it already on.

Reading the card

Each block is one substitution. On the left is the clock: Q2 8:00 means with 8:00 left in the second period. To its right, ▼ lists who is coming off. Underneath are the five who are on the floor for that stretch, and the underlined names are the ones who just came on. START is your opening five and NO SUBS means nobody changes at that break. Cut along the dashed line.

Two sizes, under Size. Pocket size is 3.45 × 5 in, two to a sheet across, and is the one that lives in a notebook. Half-sheet is 8 × 5.1 in, two down the page: the same card in bigger type on a clipboard, and it lays the stints out in two columns, so a long game stays on one card.

Changing the plan mid-game

Bench mode opens the game one stint at a time. Tap whoever is coming off and pick how long the change lasts.

This stint
A one-off swap. The printed plan picks up again at the next change.
Rest of game
Every remaining stint goes to the player you name. This is how you say “Simone takes Rafael’s minutes”.
Sit for the rest
For foul trouble, when you have no opinion about who covers. It sits that player for the rest of the game and spreads the minutes across everyone still available, counting what each has already played. Nothing already played moves, and Undo in-game changes in the top bar puts the printed plan back.
